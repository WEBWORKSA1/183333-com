United Kingdom

The UK Statutory Residence Test: Days, Ties and Traps

By the 183333 editorial teamReviewed 2026-10-269 min read

How HMRC decides UK tax residency: automatic overseas tests, automatic UK tests, and the sufficient-ties table that can make you resident on as few as 16 days.

Since April 2013 the UK has used the Statutory Residence Test (SRT). It still uses 183 days — but only as one of several automatic tests. In between the automatic tests sits the "sufficient ties" table, which is where most mobile people actually land.

Step 1: automatic overseas tests (non-resident)

You are automatically not UK resident for a tax year (6 April to 5 April) if any of these apply:

  • You were UK resident in one or more of the previous three tax years and spend fewer than 16 days in the UK.
  • You were not UK resident in any of the previous three tax years and spend fewer than 46 days in the UK.
  • You work full-time overseas, spend fewer than 91 days in the UK, and work in the UK (more than three hours) on fewer than 31 days.

Step 2: automatic UK tests (resident)

If no automatic overseas test applies, you are automatically UK resident if:

  • You spend 183 days or more in the UK in the tax year; or
  • Your only home (or one of your homes) is in the UK for a qualifying period and you are present there on at least 30 days; or
  • You work full-time in the UK for a qualifying 365-day period.

Step 3: the sufficient-ties test

If neither set of automatic tests settles it, count your UK ties:

  • Family tie — spouse/partner or minor children resident in the UK.
  • Accommodation tie — available UK accommodation used at least one night (special rules for relatives' homes).
  • Work tie — 40 or more UK workdays of more than three hours.
  • 90-day tie — more than 90 UK days in either of the previous two tax years.
  • Country tie (leavers only) — the UK is the country where you spent the most midnights.

A leaver who keeps a UK home they visit and spent more than 90 days in the UK last year already has two ties — enough to be resident on just 91 days.

Counting UK days

A day counts if you are in the UK at midnight. There is a narrow transit exception, a limited "exceptional circumstances" allowance, and a "deeming rule" that can count days without a midnight for people with three or more ties and more than 30 such days.

Split-year treatment

If you arrive or leave part-way through a tax year, split-year treatment can treat the year as two parts, with only the UK part taxed as resident. It has eight different cases with strict conditions.
