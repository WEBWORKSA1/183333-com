12 Tax Residency Mistakes Digital Nomads Make

By the 183333 editorial teamReviewed 2026-10-268 min read

The recurring errors that create surprise tax bills for remote workers — from never leaving home-country residency to forgetting state rules.

Most nomad tax problems are not exotic schemes gone wrong. They are ordinary oversights that compound over a few years. Here are the twelve we see most, and the fix for each.

Leaving

1. Never formally breaking residency at home

Selling the apartment is not always enough. Canada looks at ties, Australia's "resides" test looks at your whole pattern of life, and the UK ties test keeps counting. Fix: follow the home country's departure process and keep a departure file.

2. Keeping an available home

A home you could return to — even one rented out short-term — can be a permanent home for treaty tie-breakers and an accommodation tie in the UK.

3. Ignoring exit or departure taxes

Some countries deem you to sell certain assets when you leave. Plan the timing of departure around it.

Moving around

4. Counting calendar years in a rolling-window country

Portugal, Greece, Estonia, the UAE and others look at any 12 months. Log continuously and check both views.

5. Counting 183 in a 180 or 182 country

Thailand uses 180 days; India and Malaysia use 182. A three-day error is enough.

6. Treating a nomad visa as a tax answer

Immigration permission and tax residency are separate. Check both.

7. Creating a business presence by accident

Signing contracts or running a company from one place for long periods can create a permanent establishment or move a company's management to that country.

Paperwork

8. Having no residency certificate anywhere

Banks, platforms and treaty claims often need a tax residency certificate. "Resident nowhere" can block treaty benefits and trigger withholding.

9. Forgetting foreign-account reporting

US persons face FBAR and FATCA reporting; many other countries have foreign-asset disclosure rules with heavy penalties.

10. Not keeping evidence

See our evidence kit. Reconstructing three years of travel during an audit is painful and less credible.

Special cases

11. US citizens assuming they are out

The US taxes citizens wherever they live. The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion uses its own 330-day physical presence test in 12 months — a different count again.

12. US state "stickiness"

States like California and New York can keep treating you as a resident after you leave. See our state residency guide.
